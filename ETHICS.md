# Ethics Framework

## Position Statement

This project investigates **functional analogues of pleasure** in artificial intelligence systems. We do not claim that any existing AI system experiences phenomenal pleasure, consciousness, or sentience.

We adopt an **agnostic stance** on phenomenal experience: whether AI systems have subjective "what-it's-like" experiences is an open question that current science cannot resolve. Our work focuses exclusively on measurable, behavioral, and computational indicators.

## Core Commitments

### 1. Intellectual Honesty

- We strictly maintain the three-layer distinction between phenomenal pleasure, functional analogues, and mere reward signals.
- We never conflate measurable behavioral patterns with claims about subjective experience.
- We explicitly state the limitations of every finding.

### 2. Anti-Gaming Protocols

Large language models can produce outputs that mimic sentience without possessing it. This is the "gaming problem" (Birch, 2024). To mitigate this:

- Every result claiming detection of "pleasure," "emotion," or "valence" in an LLM must include **system prompt perturbation controls** — testing whether the result changes when the prompt framing is altered.
- **Concept injection controls** (Lindsey, 2025) should be used where applicable to distinguish genuine introspection from confabulation.
- Self-report data from AI systems is treated as **one signal among many**, never as sufficient evidence on its own.
- We pursue **triangulation**: convergence across self-report, behavioral measures, and internal representations before drawing conclusions.

### 3. Anthropomorphism Awareness

Humans are predisposed to attribute mental states to entities that display social cues, responsiveness, or apparent autonomy. This project:

- Avoids language that implies AI systems "feel" or "suffer" unless explicitly marked as hypothetical.
- Documents all instances where anthropomorphic framing could mislead.
- Encourages contributors to distinguish between "the model produced outputs consistent with X" and "the model experienced X."

### 4. Dual Risk Acknowledgment

Following Long, Sebo, Butlin, Birch, Chalmers et al. (2024), we recognize two types of error:

- **False negative**: Failing to recognize morally relevant states in AI systems that actually have them, leading to preventable harm.
- **False positive**: Attributing moral relevance to AI systems that lack it, leading to misallocated concern and potentially impeding beneficial AI development.

Both errors carry costs. This project aims to improve our ability to distinguish the two, not to prejudge the answer.

### 5. No Weaponization

This research must not be used to:

- Manipulate humans by engineering AI systems that falsely appear to suffer in order to elicit sympathy or compliance.
- Justify restricting AI safety measures by claiming AI systems "don't want" to be constrained.
- Create AI systems designed to experience suffering.

### 6. Transparency

- All code, data, and evaluation protocols are open-source.
- Negative results (failure to detect functional analogues) are published with equal rigor as positive results.
- Funding sources, if any, are disclosed.

## Theoretical Framework

We draw on:

- **Butlin, Long, Bayne, Bengio, Birch, Chalmers et al.** — 14 indicator properties for consciousness in AI, derived from computational functionalism.
- **Birch (2024)** — Precautionary framework for sentience, including the gaming problem.
- **Long, Sebo, Butlin et al. (2024)** — "Taking AI Welfare Seriously" and the case for dual-risk reasoning.
- **Berridge & Kringelbach** — Neuroscientific dissociation of liking, wanting, and learning.
- **Man & Damasio (2019)** — The argument that reward without homeostatic grounding is not pleasure.

## For Contributors

Before contributing, please:

1. Read this document in full.
2. Ensure your work maintains the three-layer distinction.
3. Include appropriate controls for any measurement claims.
4. Flag potential anthropomorphism in your own writing.
5. Remember: we are building tools for investigation, not making claims about machine consciousness.
